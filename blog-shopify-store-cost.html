How Much Does It Cost to Build a Shopify Store in 2026?

Quotes range from $500 to $50,000 - and the spread is confusing on purpose. Here's a plain-English breakdown of what you're actually paying for.

Home › Blog › How Much Does It Cost to Build a Shopify Store in 2026?

If you have asked three different people what a Shopify store setup cost is, you have probably gotten three wildly different answers - $500, $5,000, $50,000. None of them were lying. The price depends entirely on who builds it and how custom it is. Here is the honest breakdown so you can match your budget to what you actually need.

The Three Ways to Get a Shopify Store Built

1. Do it yourself with a theme ($0–$500). You pay for your Shopify subscription, maybe a paid theme ($200–$400 one time), and your own time. This works for a simple store with a handful of products and no special features. The trade-off is the learning curve and the fact that "good enough" rarely converts as well as you hope.

2. Hire a freelancer ($800–$6,000). Rates vary hugely by experience - junior developers often sit around $15–$30 per hour, intermediate developers $33–$70, and senior specialists well above that. A freelancer is great for a focused build or specific fixes. The risk is consistency and what happens after launch when they move on to the next client.

3. Work with an agency ($3,000–$50,000+). Agency or expert-level work typically runs $95–$200+ per hour, but you are not paying for hours - you are paying for strategy, design, development, and a team that owns the outcome. This is the right call when the store is core to your business and you need it to actually grow revenue, not just exist.

Where the Money Actually Goes

Most of a serious build is not "coding the website." It is the work around it: positioning and messaging, a custom design that does not look like every other store, conversion-focused product pages, a checkout that is genuinely fast, app integrations (email, reviews, subscriptions), and proper mobile optimization. Over 70% of e-commerce traffic is mobile, so a store that only looks good on desktop is leaving money on the table.

The Hidden Costs Nobody Mentions Upfront

Budget for the recurring stuff too: your monthly Shopify plan, premium apps (these add up faster than people expect), product photography, and ongoing maintenance. A beautiful store with no plan to improve it after launch slowly goes stale. The smartest founders set aside a small monthly budget for optimization, not just the one-time build.

So, Which Tier Do You Need?

If you are validating an idea, start lean - a theme or a freelancer is fine. If the store is the business and every conversion point matters, the higher tier pays for itself through a higher conversion rate. The mistake is paying agency money for a brochure, or paying freelancer money and expecting agency-level growth.
